ELECTRONIKA8

A Soviet research institute, 1975. You are the new lab assistant, and the head of the lab sends you jobs by memo. Wire up lamps, resistors, capacitors, transistors and chips on real circuit physics, and work your way up to building your own calculator.

Comrade lab assistant!

Your first job is a lamp and a battery. Your last is a calculator, built from chips you designed yourself.

Every level is a memo from the head of the lab: the director's cat is afraid of the dark, the boiler house wants an alarm that one lying sensor cannot set off, the ministry wants a counting machine on every bookkeeper's desk. You get a budget, a box of leftovers and a parts catalogue. You build the circuit on the board, switch on the power and let the test stand put it through its paces. If it passes, the memo gets its stamp and the device on the stand comes to life.

Real physics

  • The circuits run on an honest electronics simulator, a port of Paul Falstad's and Iain Sharp's circuitjs. Ohm's law is not a hint here, it is the law.
  • Parts heat up and burn. A cheap resistor goes at once, a good one turns red and smokes first. Burnt parts cost money and leave their mark on the board.
  • Measure with a multimeter, watch signals on an oscilloscope, hunt for a bad contact or a resistor with the wrong value.

From a lamp to a calculator

  • 94 levels in 10 chapters: current and lamps, Ohm's law, capacitors and time, diodes and LEDs, transistors, gates, combinational logic, memory and clocks, arithmetic, and the calculator.
  • Design your own chips. Build a gate from transistors, and its blueprint becomes a chip you can buy. Later chips are built from the chips before them.
  • Money matters. Every part has a price, and quality costs more. The flea market is cheaper and its labels are worn off. Parcels from abroad sometimes hold a dud.
  • Rise through the ranks, from lab assistant to head of the lab, with the junior researcher Petrov looking over your shoulder.

The institute around you

  • A room for every chapter, from the basement to the director's office, with Pravda on the desk and a radio playing 24 tracks.
  • A sandbox workshop with every part you have unlocked.
  • Three modes: Intern with half as much money again, Junior, and Lab head, with a single save, no starting a level over, and a failure that ends the campaign.
  • 35 awards, enamel badges handed over in their boxes, ten of them hidden.

No knowledge of electronics is needed: the game teaches it as you go.

Demo

The first two chapters, 22 levels: current and lamps, and Ohm's law. Your saves carry over to the full game, which goes on from where the demo ended.

Credits

The circuit simulation is built on circuitjs by Paul Falstad and Iain Sharp, used with the authors' permission. Sound effects come from Pixabay. How the game uses generative AI is described on its Steam page.

Privacy

ELECTRONIKA8 collects and sends no data about you. The game has no analytics, no ads and no accounts. Your progress is kept in a file on your computer (%APPDATA%\Electronika8), and Steam Cloud copies it between your machines under Steam's own terms. Achievements and your status in the friends list go through the Steam client. Questions: @dalerank.
